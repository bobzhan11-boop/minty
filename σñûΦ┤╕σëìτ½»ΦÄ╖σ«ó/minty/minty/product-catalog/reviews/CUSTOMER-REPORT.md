# Customer Experience Report: Minty Bags Storefront

## Overall impression

The merchandise is a genuine delight; the storefront it lives in is not. Across four different shoppers — a mom buying for her 7-year-old, a boutique owner scouting a supplier, a woman shopping for herself, and a first-time B2B buyer — the reaction was remarkably consistent: **"Beautiful bags, wrong shop."** Every persona fell for the products (professional photography, specific descriptions, real specs, fast pages, a working inquiry form) and every persona was jarred by the same thing: the entire site still brands itself as a **custom apparel / Print-On-Demand t-shirt factory**, even though the catalog is 96 leather-look bags.

That single mismatch — apparel branding wrapped around a bag catalog — is what repeatedly broke trust and would cost real sales.

**Overall rating: 2.5 / 5 stars** (persona ratings: 2.5, 2.5, 3, 2 — averaging to about 2.4, rounded to 2.5)

---

## What worked (every persona agreed)

- **The bags themselves are lovable.** The kids' range (pink fox crossbody, sleepy-bear with pom-pom ears, cat-ear backpack, star "girl gang" backpack, pineapple Aloha) and the women's range (quilted mini backpack, red saddle handbag, suede-look crossbody) all made shoppers want to add to cart.
- **Photography is sales-ready.** Clean, well-lit, high-resolution studio shots on neutral backgrounds. All 96 images load correctly. This is the site's biggest asset.
- **Descriptions and specs are specific and trustworthy.** Material, dimensions, colors, and SKU on every page; the copy reads like a real supplier, and the photos match the words (e.g., the "Set of 3" wristlet really shows three clutches).
- **Search mostly understands shoppers.** Style, type, and color terms return rich, relevant results (backpack 22, crossbody 44, cute 49, mermaid 3); nonsense queries correctly return nothing.
- **The inquiry path works end-to-end.** Every persona successfully submitted a quote request (HTTP 200, "Inquiry received"), with a reassuring 6-hour response promise.

---

## What felt off, per persona

**Mom shopping for kids (2.5/5)** — Fell in love with the kids' bags, but couldn't see a single price, faced an MOQ of 1000, and had no way to buy just one bag. The homepage never even hints that kids' bags exist (the featured grid is all women's bags), and searching "unicorn" — the #1 thing a little girl wants — returned zero results at her most excited moment.

**Wholesale / boutique buyer (2.5/5)** — Loved the catalog and specs, but trust collapsed on the details: the whole site sells "custom apparel" with apparel case studies and apparel clients (C&A, TAKKO, Tally Weijl); the quote form's "Interested in" dropdown only offers T-Shirts / Hoodies / Caps / Activewear — she literally cannot say she wants a bag. No phone, no factory address, and a fake WhatsApp number (+86 138 0000 0000) killed confidence in wiring a deposit.

**Fashion shopper buying for herself (3/5)** — Called it "beautiful merchandise trapped inside a storefront that still thinks it sells t-shirts." Rated highest because the product experience delighted her most, but was tripped up by hidden pricing, a broken newsletter signup, and over-broad color search ("pink" returned 34 of 96 bags, many not actually pink).

**First-time B2B visitor (2/5)** — Rated lowest. Hit the contradiction instantly: an apparel hero and title sitting directly above a featured grid of backpacks and totes. The "Mia" chat calls itself an "apparel concierge" and coaches you to search "hoodie" — which returns zero results. Read like a "hijacked or half-finished template."

---

## Top friction points that would lose a sale

1. **Identity crisis: the site sells apparel, the catalog sells bags.** Hero, page titles ("— Minty Apparel"), meta descriptions, ticker ("tees, hoodies, caps & activewear"), About page, showcase, and footer all talk apparel/POD. This is the single most-cited, trust-breaking issue across all four personas. *(critical)*
2. **The quote form can't accept a bag inquiry.** The "Interested in" dropdown offers only apparel categories — no Bags, Backpack, Crossbody, or Kids option. Buyers feel the form wasn't built for them. *(critical)*
3. **No prices anywhere.** Every product says "Contact for pricing" with empty price tiers — no ranges, no "from $X at 1000 pcs." Shoppers can't compare, budget, or justify an inquiry. *(high)*
4. **Hard MOQ of 1000 with no sample or small-batch path.** Retail shoppers can't buy one bag; boutiques wanting 100-300 units or a paid sample have nowhere to go. It also contradicts the homepage's "Flexible MOQ" claim. *(high)*
5. **Weak trust signals for B2B.** No phone, no factory address, a placeholder WhatsApp number, and apparel-oriented certs/clients with nothing downloadable to verify. *(high)*
6. **The "Mia" apparel concierge sends shoppers down a dead end** by suggesting they search "hoodie" (0 results). *(high)*
7. **Broken newsletter and promotions** (both return 501 Not Implemented) — dead ends that make the site feel half-finished. *(low–medium)*

---

## Top improvements that would make customers buy

1. **Rebrand the whole site to bags / leather goods** — hero, page title suffix ("— Minty Bags"), meta, ticker, About story, factory stats, showcase, footer, and product descriptions. Reference the real company (Pingyang Jinweilong / HOKA GROUP). *This one fix addresses the biggest complaint from every persona.*
2. **Fix the "Interested in" dropdown** to bag categories (Women's Bags, Kids' Bags, Backpacks, Crossbody, Totes, Clutches, Private Label, Bulk Wholesale) and auto-select the category when the inquiry comes from a product page.
3. **Add price guidance** — at minimum indicative ranges or "from $X at MOQ 1000," or state clearly "wholesale only, min 1000 units" up front so retail shoppers self-qualify instead of getting frustrated.
4. **Clarify the ordering model** — make the wholesale/B2B nature obvious, and add a real sample-order path (cost, lead time, how to request) so boutiques and curious shoppers have a next step.
5. **Strengthen trust details** — real factory address (Pingyang, Zhejiang), a working phone/WhatsApp, business license reference, and bag-relevant compliance (REACH/RoHS/Prop 65, LWG) with downloadable certs.
6. **Rewrite Mia** as a bag concierge using a working example keyword ("backpack" / "tote").
7. **Surface kids' bags on the homepage** (feature a few, or add a "Shop Kids' Bags" entry point) so moms know the range exists.
8. **Polish the small stuff** — add a dedicated color filter (so "pink" returns pink bags), map whimsical kid searches (unicorn/rainbow/princess) to friendly suggestions instead of an empty screen, and either implement or hide the newsletter/promotions features.

---

**Bottom line:** Customers love the bags and trust the product pages, but the apparel branding, the apparel-only inquiry form, and the total absence of pricing make them doubt they're even in the right store. Fix the identity and the "how do I buy this" path, and this jumps from a hesitant 2.5 to a confident 4+.
